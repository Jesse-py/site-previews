Tuning basics

Questions we hear

How much does it cost to have a piano tuned?

The cost to tune a piano is $100.00. Most tunings take about one hour.

Why does a piano go out of tune?

A piano has more than 230 strings with a combined 18 to 20 tons of tension, held by the cast iron plate, the wood frame, pinblock, soundboard and bridges. Anything that changes the size or position of those wood parts changes the tension.

Weather is the main reason: as temperature and humidity change, the wood expands and contracts. Strings stretch too, which is why a new piano, or a new string on an older piano, needs tuning more often until the tension settles.

Can every piano be tuned properly?

Most pianos can, if the structure is still in good condition. Loose tuning pins, often from a cracked or dried-out pinblock, can keep a piano from holding its tune, and that's difficult to fix.

What is pitch raising or lowering?

When a piano is a quarter step or more flat (or sharp) of A=440, usually because it hasn't been tuned in several years or sits where humidity swings a lot each season, each note has to be tuned more than once in the same visit so the tension is stable at the end.

How far ahead should I book?

Most of the time we need one to two weeks' notice. It can be less in summer, and more during the holiday season between Thanksgiving and Christmas.

Buying a used piano?

Have it checked first: if the structural parts aren't sound, it won't hold a tune. And a rebuilt piano can beat a new one for less money, because wood dried in the early 1900s was far better than today's kiln-dried wood. If we were buying for ourselves, we'd look for an early 1900s Mason & Hamlin or Steinway that has been rebuilt. Ask for an evaluation.
